## Overview

The Beast of Bray Road is a cryptid reported in and around Elkhorn, Walworth County, Wisconsin, with most sightings clustering in the late 1980s and early 1990s. Witnesses describe a 6- to 7-foot-tall wolf-headed creature, covered in fur, with glowing red or yellow-orange eyes, sometimes seen standing or running on its hind legs and sometimes on all fours. Reports of contact with vehicles include claw marks on doors. Reporter Linda Godfrey, then writing for the Walworth County Week, was assigned to cover the story and produced a series of articles that became the 1992–1993 'werewolf of Elkhorn' coverage and her 2003 book The Beast of Bray Road. Godfrey, initially skeptical, became a long-time investigator of related 'manwolf' reports across Wisconsin, Michigan and the Midwest. Skeptics attribute the reports to misidentified gray wolves, large feral dogs, mange-affected bears, and a folklore feedback loop reinforced by a 2005 Asylum horror film and other media.

- **Origin**: Named for Bray Road, a rural road near Elkhorn in Walworth County, Wisconsin, where multiple sightings clustered in the late 1980s and early 1990s.
- **Meaning**: Literally, the beast of Bray Road; 'Bray' is the family name commemorated by the road, not a description of the creature.
- **First use**: Around 1991–1992 in the Walworth County Week newspaper coverage by Linda Godfrey

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Manwolf / dogman cryptid** _(Linda Godfrey)_

Linda Godfrey's hypothesis that the Beast of Bray Road is part of a broader, possibly paranormal 'manwolf' phenomenon spanning the Upper Midwest.

### Skeptical

**Misidentified canid or bear** _(Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources biologists, Mainstream skeptics)_

Witnesses likely misidentified gray wolves, coyote-wolf hybrids, large feral dogs (Great Pyrenees, Newfoundland), or mange-affected bears, especially in low light.

**Folklore feedback loop** _(Folklorists of contemporary legend)_

Once the 'werewolf of Elkhorn' story entered local newspapers and pop culture, similar reports proliferated, reinforced by the 2005 Asylum film and ongoing cryptid media.
